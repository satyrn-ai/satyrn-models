Foundations

Principles

Six rules. Everything else in this document follows from them.

Warm, not corporate

Cream and ink, not white and grey. The palette is the friendliest thing about a project that is otherwise a pile of training scripts, and it is deliberate. Never substitute a neutral grey ramp for the warm one.

Gold is punctuation

One accent, used sparingly — primary action, active state, focus ring, links. If two things on a screen are competing for gold, one of them is wrong.

Colour is never the only signal

This matters more here than in most systems, because the brand hue is amber and so is the warning colour. Every status carries a glyph and a word. See Semantic.

Both themes are designed

Dark is not an inverted light theme. Each semantic role has its own step per theme because no single hue clears contrast on both cream and near-black.

Numbers get typographic care

Training and validation loss, token counts, durations. Tabular figures, right alignment, constant precision. A metric column that jitters while a run streams is a bug.

Show the machine honestly

Local-first means the user can see what ran. Prefer real commands, real log lines and real numbers over reassuring abstractions. An opaque spinner is a missed opportunity to show what is happening.

These express the project's open-source, community-owned and local-first intent. The product design owns those commitments.

Foundations

Colour

Every swatch below is read live from the stylesheet and its contrast is computed in the browser. Click any swatch to copy its value.

Brand

These eleven values define the repository's visual identity. The cream/ink pair anchors the surfaces and the orange→gold accent carries emphasis: orange in the light theme, gold in the dark one.

The accent rule. Use --accent-ui for backgrounds, borders, focus rings and icons — it only needs 3:1. For accent text — links, inline emphasis — use --accent-text, which resolves to a darkened orange in light theme and the gold in dark. Brand orange on cream is 3.16:1 and fails as body text.

Warm neutral ramp

Ten steps carrying almost all surface area. The hue band sits at 35–42°, keeping the neutral ramp warm and visually related to the orange and gold accents. Steps 50–300 are surfaces and borders; 600–900 are ink on cream; 200–400 are ink on the dark theme.

Ink roles — light

Ink roles — dark

Step 500 is the hinge: it is the lightest step that still clears 3:1 on cream, which makes it --border-strong in both themes — the one step that does the same job on either surface. Step 600 is the lightest usable ink on cream at 4.68:1; anything lighter fails as text.

Foundations

Semantic & status

Four roles, each with a light step and a dark step. Both are chosen to clear 4.5:1 against their own theme's page and card surface, because these colours carry words and numbers, not decoration.

The amber problem

This is the most consequential constraint in the whole system, and it is unfixable by colour choice alone. Satyrn's accent occupies the amber band — orange at 27° and gold at 44°. Warning is conventionally amber too. To clear 4.5:1 on a cream page an amber must be dark, and a dark amber is the brand brown-orange.

Therefore, three hard rules. One — every warning carries an icon and a word, never a bare colour. Two — the brand accent is never used to carry status; a gold chip does not mean "caution". Three — links inside a status surface take that status's own colour, underlined, not the brand accent.

Reserved meanings

Do

  • Keep green and red exclusively for outcome — passed/failed, improved/regressed, healthy/broken.
  • Pair every status colour with an icon and a text label.
  • Use amber for degraded-but-working: a run that is slow, a checkpoint that is stale, a dataset with skipped rows.
  • Use blue for neutral information that needs no action.

Don't

  • Reuse green or red as a chart series colour — it will read as an outcome.
  • Signal run state with colour alone in a table cell.
  • Use the brand gold for a warning badge, however tempting the hue is.
  • Use pure #FF0000; it vibrates on ink and fails on cream.

Typography

Fredoka sets the voice at heading sizes; Inter does the work everywhere else; JetBrains Mono handles anything that must align by character or be copied and run.

Fredoka

Display & headings. Rounded, warm, a little playful — it is what stops the palette reading as a bank. Weight 600 only. All headings, h1 through h6.

Inter

UI & body. Exceptional at small sizes, and the source of the tabular figures used for every metric. Weights 400–700.

JetBrains Mono

Code & identifiers. Commands, run IDs, checkpoint hashes, file paths, log output. Not for metrics — see Numerics.

Fredoka is fetched at 600 and only 600. Requesting 500 or 700 snaps to the nearest declared weight anyway and pulls a second font file for no visible difference. Pin font-weight: 600 wherever the display face is applied.

Tracking

Letter-spacing scales inversely with size. Fredoka is a rounded face with generous sidebearings, so it needs less negative tracking than a grotesque at the same size — a display scale borrowed from an Inter-headed system will look pinched here.

Do

  • --track-display (−0.025em) at 36px and above.
  • --track-heading (−0.015em) between 22 and 32px.
  • --track-caps (+0.06em) on uppercase labels.
  • Use text-wrap: balance on headings.

Don't

  • Apply negative tracking to body text or anything under 18px.
  • Use uppercase for anything longer than three words.
  • Set Fredoka below 18px — the rounding stops reading and it just looks soft.
  • Mix Fredoka and Inter within a single line.
Foundations

Numerics

Training results are numbers work. Inter's tabular figures — not a monospace font — are the correct tool for a metric.

Proportional figures — wrong

1.11118.88881.81888.1118

Every row holds the same five digits, yet no two are the same width — a 1 is far narrower than an 8. The marks show where each row starts: they scatter. A loss ticking from 8 to 1 physically resizes the column.

Tabular figures — right

1.11118.88881.81888.1118

The same five digits on a fixed advance width. Equal digit counts produce equal widths, the marks form a straight line, and a streaming metric never moves the column.

.num {
  text-align: right;
  font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums;
  font-feature-settings: "tnum";  /* fallback for older engines */
}

Do

  • Right-align every numeric column, including its header.
  • Hold precision constant down a column — 0.4210, not 0.421.
  • Use JetBrains Mono for run IDs, checkpoint hashes and file paths.
  • Mark absent data with an em dash, never a zero — missing validation loss is not zero loss.

Don't

  • Set metrics in a monospace font — it is wider and slower to scan.
  • Centre numbers, ever.
  • Let precision vary row to row.
  • Abbreviate to 1.2M tokens where the exact count is the point.
Foundations

Space & radius

A 4px base grid. Every padding, margin and gap is a token — no arbitrary pixel values.

The rem trap. This document runs at a 16px root. A consuming interface may use a different root size, so the px column above is authoritative. Convert the values deliberately rather than copying rem values across root contexts.

Radius

Radius encodes scale: the larger the surface, the softer the corner. Named by role rather than size, so a button is always --radius-control regardless of what that value becomes.

Foundations

Surfaces & containment

Containment is a ladder, not a default. Every box you draw is a line the eye has to process, so use the lowest rung that does the job — and never stack two rungs to say one thing.

The ladder

The same block of content at each level. Nearly all UI belongs on rungs 0 and 1.

The card surfaces barely exist — and that is fine, conditionally

The values in this book make the raised card almost the same luminance as the page it sits on in both themes. A card is therefore bounded primarily by its low-contrast border.

Surface pairThemeSeparationReading

For a static content card that only groups related copy, that is acceptable. WCAG 1.4.11 governs user-interface components and meaningful graphics; a decorative grouping box is neither, and the near-invisible fill is doing exactly the gentle job it should.

For anything interactive — an input, a clickable run row, a selectable checkpoint, a button with only a border — it is not. Those take --border-strong, which is the ramp's 500 step and clears 3:1 in both themes.

The one-signal rule

A border says "this is a region." A fill says the same thing. A shadow says it a third time. Pick one. The most common way an interface gets heavy is not too much content — it is three signals fighting to bound the same rectangle.

Do

  • Start at rung 0. Let a heading and whitespace group things.
  • Add a hairline only where two blocks genuinely abut with no room between.
  • Reserve a border or fill for regions that scroll, accept input, or can be dismissed.
  • Swap signals across themes rather than stacking: a border on cream, a fill on ink.

Don't

  • Put a filled surface inside a bordered one — that is two rungs for one boundary.
  • Give a card a border, a fill, and a resting shadow.
  • Add a hover shadow to something that is not interactive. Motion and lift must not imply an action that does not exist.
  • Wrap each of filters, chart and table in its own panel when one page already contains them.

One deliberate exception: alerts and badges carry a tinted fill and a matching border. There the colour is semantic rather than structural — the pair reinforces meaning, not containment.

Elevation tiers

Rung 4 in detail. In light theme lift is a warm-tinted shadow; on a near-black surface a shadow is nearly invisible, so lift is carried by surface lightness instead. Toggle the theme to see both.

Identity

Voice & tone

This section makes the Satyrn Models voice explicit so it remains consistent across contributors and interfaces.

Plain and unhurried

Short sentences. Ordinary words. No adjective stacking, no "revolutionary", no "seamless". "We got tired of choosing" is the register.

Dry, not jokey

The humour is understated and comes from precision, not from puns. "The consequences are pleasantly boring." Never a wink, never an exclamation mark in body copy.

Honest about what isn't done

Say "Still being written" rather than using vague launch language. Unfinished and labelled beats finished-sounding and false.

First person plural, addressing you

"We" build it, "you" run it. "From us, for us." Never "users", never the passive voice hiding who decided something.

Argue from the concrete

Not "unparalleled transparency" but "when something behaves strangely you can read the code that did it." Claims land as examples.

Never sell fear

Local-first is argued on its merits — trains, outages, bills — not by implying the alternative is spying on you. Confidence, not paranoia.

In practice

Error and empty-state copy

The same voice applies where it is hardest. An error names what happened, what it means, and the next move — in that order, without apology or blame.

Do

  • "No checkpoint at runs/2026-08-14/. Training may still be on its first save."
  • "Dataset path is missing. Choose a readable JSONL file."
  • Name the file, the flag or the command the reader needs next.

Don't

  • "Oops! Something went wrong 😅"
  • "Invalid input." — invalid how, and which input?
  • "We're sorry for the inconvenience." Fix the sentence, drop the apology.

The mark

The identity has three pieces, and they are not interchangeable.

The mark

The ringed planet, drawn as a balloon on a string. The everyday identity: header, favicon, footer, avatars, anywhere small.

The mascot

The full illustration in its gold-ringed medallion. Reserved for hero moments — the landing page, a release announcement, a conference slide.

Satyrn

The wordmark

Set in Fredoka 600, and only Fredoka 600. Pairs with the mark to the left of it; never set inside the medallion.

The mark and the mascot were hand-illustrated for Satyrn by Georgi Ker. They are not stock, not clip art and not generated. Treat them the way you would treat someone's code contribution: credit them, do not redraw them, and do not feed them to a tool that will produce a “variant”. If a new size or crop is needed, commission it.

Attribution

The credit is part of the artwork, not an optional footnote. Wherever the mascot appears at feature size — a landing page, a release post, a conference deck, a README hero — the credit goes with it, and the name links to georgiker.com.

the credit line, verbatim
Artwork by the fabulous <a href="https://georgiker.com/">Georgi Ker</a>

“The fabulous” is deliberate — it is the voice doing its job in the one place most projects default to a bare legal line. Do not trim it to “Artwork: G. Ker”.

Do

  • Carry the credit anywhere the mascot appears at feature size.
  • Link the name; an unlinked credit is half a credit.
  • Underline it — it sits inside a sentence, where colour alone does not mark a link.

Don't

  • Drop the credit because the mark is “just an icon” in the header — the footer credit covers the page, so keep one per page.
  • Abbreviate or reword the line.
  • Reuse the artwork in a context you would not run past Georgi Ker first.

Sizes

The mark holds down to about 24px; do not render it below that size because the ring and string collapse into a blob. The mascot has far more detail and needs 120px or more before its face resolves; under that, use the mark.

The halo, and why it exists

The balloon string is a thin dark stroke with nothing behind it. On the ink surface it all but vanishes and the planet reads as floating. A two-pass parchment drop-shadow restores it without altering the artist's file — and it is a token, so every placement picks it up. Both plates below are fixed colours, so you can see the two treatments at once without toggling the theme.

The mascot is exempt. Its medallion carries its own cream field, so it is already sitting on light ground whatever the page behind it is doing — applying the halo there would only fringe the outer gold ring.

Clear space

Keep clear space of at least the planet's radius on every side. Nothing — type, rules, edges, other logos — enters the dashed zone.

Do

  • Reuse the one mark file across both themes and let --mark-filter add the halo on dark.
  • Give the mark aspect-ratio: 1 so it reserves its box before it loads and does not shove the wordmark sideways.
  • Keep clear space of at least the planet's radius on every side.
  • Use the mark below 120px and the mascot above it.
  • Carry the Georgi Ker credit, linked, wherever the mascot appears at feature size.
  • Give the mark an empty alt when the wordmark beside it already names the project — otherwise a screen reader says "Satyrn" twice.

Don't

  • Ship separate light and dark artwork files. A display: none image is still downloaded, and the old pair had different internal padding, rendering one ~40% smaller at identical CSS widths.
  • Recolour the planet. Gold is the only planet.
  • Redraw, trace, re-crop or regenerate the artwork. Commission it instead.
  • Put the mascot on a dark plate expecting it to knock out — it brings its own cream field.
  • Set the wordmark in anything but Fredoka 600.
  • Stretch, rotate, outline or add effects to either image.

Community use

Satyrn is community-owned, which makes the mark a shared asset rather than a corporate one — and that changes the guidance. A future code licence would not by itself define use of the name or logo, so those terms would need to be stated separately.

Forks, meetups, talks, stickers, blog posts and criticism may use the name and mark to refer to the project. What they may not do is imply that they are the project, or that the project endorses them. A fork should say “based on Satyrn”, not ship as Satyrn. This is the standard open-source position and it exists to keep the name meaning one thing.

This section states design intent, not legal policy. If this repository adopts a trademark policy, link it here rather than restating it.

Components

Buttons

Five variants. Exactly one primary button per view — it is the answer to "what does this screen want me to do?"

HTML
<button class="btn btn-primary btn-md">Start training run</button>
<button class="btn btn-secondary btn-md">Read the docs</button>
<button class="btn btn-neutral btn-md">Export dataset</button>
<button class="btn btn-ghost btn-md">Cancel</button>
<button class="btn btn-danger btn-md">Delete checkpoint</button>
Why the primary fill is deep orange in both themes. The dark theme's accent is gold, but white on gold is 1.8:1 and ink on gold is 9.5:1 — a gold button needs dark text, and a dark-on-amber button reads as a warning. Deep orange takes white at 4.86:1, clearing the 4.5:1 normal-text requirement for every button size. Gold stays the accent for links, focus and active state.

Sizes & states

Do

  • Label with a verb phrase — "Start training run", not "Submit".
  • Keep the disabled reason visible nearby.
  • Preserve button width while loading to avoid layout shift.
  • Keep every target at least 24×24px, per WCAG 2.2.

Don't

  • Put two primary buttons in one view.
  • Use danger styling for anything reversible.
  • Fill a button with brand gold.
  • Drop below 32px height.
Components

Command blocks

A local-first tool is adopted one pasted command at a time, so command blocks deserve the same care as primary actions.

The text below is illustrative component content, not an executable setup contract. Use the corpus-builder and trainer READMEs for current commands.

install
satyrn-tool …
run a task
satyrn-tool --option value
The prompt is never in the copyable text. The $ is generated by CSS ::before with user-select: none. Typed into the markup it travels to the clipboard and the pasted command fails — the single most common defect in this component across the whole industry.

Do

  • One command per block. If it needs three, it needs three blocks or a script.
  • Put a copy button on every block, and confirm the copy with a toast.
  • Label the block with what the command achieves, not the shell it runs in.
  • In product documentation, source real commands from the owning package README rather than copying them into a design artifact.

Don't

  • Include the $ or > prompt in the copyable text.
  • Wrap a long command — let it scroll horizontally. A wrapped command hides a flag.
  • Pipe a remote script into a shell as the headline install. Satyrn's whole argument is that you can read what you run.
  • Interleave output inside the command block; that is a log block.

Forms

Neutral by default, accent on focus, and errors that explain rather than accuse.

All fields, model names, stages, capacities and values in this specimen are fabricated presentation data. They do not assert supported runtime configuration.

Use concise help text for the format accepted by the owning contract.

Errors use aria-invalid plus aria-describedby so screen readers announce the message — the red border alone is not an accessible error state. Note the error names the constraint and the fix, per the voice rules.

Components

Badges & run status

Compact state labels. Always text, always an icon where the state is an outcome — a bare coloured dot is not a badge, and in this palette it is genuinely ambiguous.

Every one of these carries a glyph as well as a hue. Strip the colour and the meaning survives; that is the test.

Run-status presentation vocabulary

When a runtime contract defines one of these states, present it consistently across UI surfaces. This table defines visual meaning, not a pipeline state machine.

StateRoleMeans
QueuedNeutralAccepted, not started. No resources held.
RunningInfoIn progress. Pair with a progress bar and a step count.
CompletedSuccessFinished, artifacts written.
DegradedWarningFinished, but something was skipped, retried or truncated. Say what.

Prefer this compact vocabulary unless an owning runtime contract requires another state. More detailed progress belongs in supporting text.

Components

Alerts

Bold lead, then the detail. Every alert carries an icon so the meaning survives without colour — mandatory here, given the amber problem.

Paths, flags, counts and recovery actions below are fabricated copy specimens. Actual recovery guidance must come from the affected contract or package README.

Run complete. 3 epochs in 4h 12m. Checkpoint written to runs/2026-08-16/final/.
Task stopped at step 1,204. Name the failed operation and link to the recovery action defined by its contract.
412 examples skipped. They exceeded the 4,096-token window. The run continued on the remaining 47,798.
Running on CPU. No CUDA device was found, so this will be slow. Nothing left your machine.

The link inside the error alert is red and underlined, not gold. A brand-accent link inside a status surface puts two ambers 13.5° apart on the same line.

Components

Loading & empty states

Use a skeleton when you know the shape of what's coming, a spinner when you don't, and a progress bar whenever you actually know the denominator — which, in a training pipeline, is most of the time.

Skeleton

Table rows, cards, known layouts.

Spinner

Only where the duration is genuinely unknowable.

Progress

Step 1,280 / 2,000 · epoch 2 of 3 · 38m left

Preferred. Show the step, not just the bar.

No runs yet

Nothing has been trained in this directory.

No checkpoints match

Nothing matches “step>5000”.

An empty state always names the cause and offers the next action. “No data” on its own is a dead end. Distinguish nothing exists yet from nothing matched — they need different buttons.

Run & training-result tables

A pattern for dense run data: tabular figures, sticky headers, and improvement shown by arrow and colour.

Every identifier, stage, status, count and metric below is fabricated presentation data.

Log output

Log output is a first-class visual surface, not a debug afterthought. The same semantic roles carry it.

The specimen is fabricated. Current CLI logging and file behavior is owned by the package contracts.

In a terminal

For a future terminal surface that supports semantic theming, map roles onto the terminal's ANSI slots. Exact output, prefixes, streams and colour-disable behavior belong in that CLI's runtime contract.

Text labels preserve meaning without colour. Treat NO_COLOR, TTY detection and stream selection as implementation requirements only when the owning contract adopts them.

Do

  • Keep the timestamp, level and message in fixed columns so the eye can skip to the level.
  • Let the log scroll horizontally rather than wrapping a path.
  • Colour the level token, not the whole line — a fully red line is unreadable.
  • Keep screen and file presentation consistent when the owning contract requires both.

Don't

  • Use the brand gold for a log level.
  • Animate, pulse or auto-scroll away from something the reader is reading.
  • Hide errors behind a collapsed section.
  • Rely on animated progress as the only signal that work is active.
Patterns

Charts

Three palettes for three jobs. Each was validated for hue separation, brand-band avoidance and contrast against both surfaces, rather than picked by eye.

Categorical — identity

Up to six runs or configurations compared side by side. Assign in fixed order, slot 1 first; never cycle or generate a seventh hue — fold the remainder into “Other”. Green and red are absent by design: they are reserved for outcome, and a green loss curve would read as “good”.

py314-sft-04 py314-sft-03 py314-sft-02 baseline py315-cpt-01 Other runs

No slot lands in the brand's 35–55° gold band, so a series can never be mistaken for the accent. The worst pair in the set separates by 48.5° of hue.

Sequential — magnitude

One hue, light to dark, for attention maps, token-frequency heatmaps and density. Lightness carries the value, so it survives greyscale printing and colour-blind reading alike.

The lightest step is close to the cream page by construction — that is what "low value" should look like. On a light background give small cells a 1px --border so an empty cell and a low-value cell stay distinguishable.

Diverging — polarity

Δ against a baseline: which runs improved, which regressed. Two poles with a neutral warm midpoint — never a hue in the middle.

A diverging midpoint is deliberately near the page colour: "no change" should recede. That means the mid-band drops below 3:1 against the surface, which is correct for a heatmap cell but wrong for a thin line — always pair a diverging cell with its numeric value.

Do

  • Show a legend for two or more series, and direct-label up to four.
  • Keep grid lines recessive — --border, 1px, no fill.
  • Offer a table view of any chart's underlying data.
  • Set axis values and labels in ink tokens, not the series colour.
  • Start a loss axis at a sensible floor, and say so — a truncated axis that is not labelled is a lie.

Don't

  • Build a dual-axis chart. Use two charts, or index to a common base.
  • Let a filter repaint the surviving series — colour follows the run, not its rank.
  • Use a rainbow scale for magnitude.
  • Smooth a loss curve without saying you did.
  • Label every point; label the ones that carry the argument.
Patterns

Accessibility

Targets, not aspirations. These are checkable, and this page checks its own colours against them on every theme change.

Contrast

Body text ≥ 4.5:1. Large text (≥18.66px bold or ≥24px) and non-text UI — borders, icons, focus rings — ≥ 3:1. Every ink and semantic token here clears its bar against both the page and the card surface of its own theme.

Focus

Every interactive element takes a 2px --accent-ui outline at 2px offset via :focus-visible. Never remove the outline without replacing it with something at least as visible.

Never colour alone

The load-bearing rule in this system, because the brand hue and the warning hue are neighbours. Outcome carries an arrow, status carries an icon and a word, series carry a legend and direct labels.

Target size

WCAG 2.2 asks for 24×24px minimum. Inline links inside a sentence are exempt; standalone nav links, tabs and icon buttons are not, and must be padded to clear it.

Link affordance

Gold on parchment is 1.48:1 — far under the 3:1 needed when colour alone marks a link. Any link inside a run of body text is underlined. Links in a nav row may rely on position instead.

Motion

Interaction transitions are ≤ 200 ms. Loading spinners and skeletons may use longer repeating cycles, but prefers-reduced-motion collapses them to one near-instant cycle. Monitoring views do not pulse continuously — motion must not compete with the data it presents.
